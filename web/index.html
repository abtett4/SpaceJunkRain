      What the marks mean. An amber trace follows a reference orbit built from the object’s real inclination and perigee/apogee; its direction and phase are illustrative, and it does not model the descent or claim a reentry location. For most objects that orbit is the one GCAT records for it, dated and often from its working life rather than its last days, so the card says when it was recorded. Tiangong-1 at short windows replays its last known orbit (SGP4). A ring marks an object with no usable orbit, at a stable random point on the globe chosen evenly by area — not a reentry location. Launches are every orbital launch attempt in GCAT, shown as a ring at the launch pad (or at the launch site when GCAT has no pad coordinates); failed attempts are smaller and red. Dragon CRS-14 (April 2018) additionally has an illustrative ascent arc into its first recorded orbit; it is not a flight reconstruction.

      Times. About 4,100 reentries have a time of day from GCAT, used only when GCAT and Space-Track agree on the day. The rest give only the day. Those objects get a stable, randomly assigned time within that UTC day; it is an animation anchor, not an estimated reentry time. Launch times keep GCAT’s precision, usually to the minute or second; the few given only to the day get an assigned time the same way.

      About the data

      • Decay dates come from the SATCAT and Space-Track's historical decay messages, which record each one to the day only; about 4,100 also have a time of day from GCAT, used only when both sources agree on the day. Each dot sits on its reported day. When only the date is known, a reproducible random time within that UTC day drives the timeline and orbit replay; it is not an estimated reentry time. Within a row, dots are spread up and down only so they don't overlap.
      • A "source" is everything from one launch (its international designator, e.g. 1999-025). Debris usually keeps its parent's designator, so a breakup shows up as a burst within one source.
      • Launches are every orbital launch attempt in J. McDowell's GCAT (planet4589.org, CC BY 4.0), placed at the launch pad GCAT gives, or at the launch site when it gives no pad.
      • "Still in orbit" counts every tracked object, working satellites included. Only tracked objects appear here, roughly softball-sized and up in low orbit. Collision dates and altitudes are hand-curated.
