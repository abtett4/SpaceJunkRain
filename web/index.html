    About the data

    • Decay dates come from the SATCAT and Space-Track's historical decay messages, which record each one to the day only. Each dot sits on its reported day. When only the date is known, a reproducible random time within that UTC day drives the timeline and orbit replay; it is not an estimated reentry time. Within a row, dots are spread up and down only so they don't overlap.
    • A "source" is everything from one launch (its international designator, e.g. 1999-025). Debris usually keeps its parent's designator, so a breakup shows up as a burst within one source.
    • "Still in orbit" counts every tracked object, working satellites included. Only tracked objects appear here, roughly softball-sized and up in low orbit. Collision dates and altitudes are hand-curated.
